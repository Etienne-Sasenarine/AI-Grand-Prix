# The first live smoke run, 20 Sep 2026: what it found and what it was

`scripts/smoke_test.py` ran against live PhysX for the first time on a RunPod
RTX 6000 Ada after the setup in `RUNBOOK_RUNPOD.md`. Section 1 is the run as
it was read at the time: 10 pass, 4 fail. Section 2 is the resolution. Two of
the four failures were what they looked like; the other two were not, and
chasing them turned up two further bugs that nothing was looking for.

Final state: **15 of 15 checks pass**, verified.

---

## 1. As read at the time: 10 pass, 4 fail

### 1.1 The aircraft weighs five times too much: critical

```
BODY_NAMES:    ['body', 'prop1', 'prop2', 'prop3', 'prop4']
PER_BODY_MASS: [1.745, 1.745, 1.745, 1.745, 1.745]
TOTAL:         8.725 kg
```

`assets/five_in_drone.py` set `mass_props=sim_utils.MassPropertiesCfg(mass=1.745)`
on the spawn. That applies to every rigid body in the articulation, so the
four propeller links each weighed a full airframe. `stick_to_newtons` sizes
thrust for 1.745 kg; against 8.725 kg that is a thrust-to-weight of 0.2. Every
episode was a brick falling, and every training run since the mass change had
been learning against it.

### 1.2 Inertia products are not zero

```
[FAIL] inertia products are zero    max |off-diagonal| = 4.70e-04
```

`set_body_inertia` wrote the diagonal and left the off-diagonal terms alone,
assuming they were zero. Against a diagonal of ~0.004 they were ~12%.

### 1.3 Inertia diagonal is right, the tolerance was too tight

```
[FAIL] PhysX inertia matches the estimate  (0.0039, 0.0055, 0.0076) vs (0.004, 0.0055, 0.0075)
```

Float32 round-tripping through PhysX, not a mismatch; the check used an
absolute `< 1e-6`.

### 1.4 Some environment gets zero collective at zero action

```
[FAIL] zero action gives a plausible collective   0.000..1.071 g
```

The upper end is right. The `0.000` was suspected to be `_applied_thrust_n`
still zero from reset when the check read it.

### What passed

The uncertain ones, and they hold up: hover points sample 0.210–0.289 across
`PLANT_HOVER_RANGE`; thrust-to-weight samples 4.29–7.98 across
`PLANT_TWR_RANGE`; the plant genuinely varies between environments (hover
spread 0.079); the thrust curve stays convex (quad_share 0.051–0.871); action
delays are 0–2 steps with three distinct values across envs; the rate filter
leaves real lag (alpha 0.170–0.632); the observation width is **1760** (v2,
with action history, as the contract says); 200 steps produce finite
observations. So latency modelling, plant randomisation, the thrust curve and
the v2 observation all worked on real hardware. The failures were all in the
asset's mass and inertia, which is where the smoke test was pointed.

## 2. What each turned out to be

### 2.1 The mass: confirmed, fixed

Every piece was individually correct: the contract said 1.745, the thrust
curve was built for 1.745, the asset config said 1.745. Only the *product* of
the config and the asset's link count was wrong, and nothing looked at that
product.

**Fixed** by removing `mass_props` from the spawn entirely and distributing
mass at startup in `events.set_body_mass`, where the links can be told apart.
The airframe takes whatever the props leave, so the total is arithmetic rather
than three numbers maintained by hand: revising the 5 g prop estimate moves
mass between links but can never change what the aircraft weighs.

1.745 kg is all-up competition weight **with props fitted**, confirmed with the
person who weighed it. The props therefore come out of that number, not on top
of it; the other reading gives 1.765 kg and is the plausible way to "fix" this
wrongly later, so a test pins it. An unmatched link raises rather than silently
keeping its spawn mass, because that is the shape the original bug had.

### 2.2 The inertia products: confirmed, but not for the reason assumed

The products were real. The assumption that `set_inertias` would clear them
was wrong, and that mattered more than the original finding.

**PhysX does not store a 3×3 inertia tensor.** It stores three principal
moments plus the rotation taking body axes to principal axes, and that
rotation lives in the *COM pose*, not in the tensor. `set_inertias` writes the
moments and leaves the rotation exactly where it was. Writing zeros into the
products does nothing at all.

The read-back gave itself away:

```
ixx  0.004054  vs 0.0040 written   +1.35%
iyy  0.005500  vs 0.0055 written    exact
izz  0.007446  vs 0.0075 written   -0.72%
```

Middle axis exact, outer two moved in opposite directions: what a rotation
about y does and essentially nothing else does. Solving it:

```
(Izz - Ixx)·sin t·cos t = 4.32e-4     ->  t = 7.1 deg
Ixx·cos²t + Izz·sin²t   = 0.004054    ->  matches
Ixx·sin²t + Izz·cos²t   = 0.007446    ->  matches
```

The COM quaternion then read `(-0.000775, 0.062293, -0.003859, 0.99805)`, a
rotation about y of **7.16°**, against 7.1° predicted from the inertia alone.
Clearing it makes the read-back exactly `(0.004, 0.0055, 0.0075)` with
products at `0.00e+00`.

A USD conversion artifact: the URDF has no products of inertia and neither
does the airframe being modelled. Not harmless, since a tilt about y couples
roll into yaw on every input. The four prop links have identity here and read
back clean, which confirmed the mechanism was this one link's authoring rather
than a limit of the API.

> **The quaternion is xyzw.** Isaac Lab's own math utilities are wxyz. Writing
> wxyz identity here stores a 180° rotation about x, which maps a diagonal
> tensor to itself, so every inertia assertion still passes while the body is
> upside down in its own mass frame. A test asserts the real part is in the
> last slot, because that is the only thing that catches it.

The test that was supposed to cover the products seeded its fixture with
zeros, so it could not distinguish "cleared" from "never touched". It now
seeds the measured tilt.

### 2.3 The inertia tolerance: a bad check, not a bad plant

Absolute `1e-6` against `4e-3` quantities making a float32 round trip. Now
relative, and the products are compared against the smallest principal moment
so the bar scales with the airframe rather than with float32.

### 2.4 The `0.000 g` env: not a bug at all, and it was hiding one

`reset()` zeroes `_applied_thrust_n` (`actions.py`), which is correct: a fresh
episode has issued no command. The smoke test read that buffer *after*
stepping, so any env that terminated mid-step scored 0 g. The plant was fine.

The check now excludes envs that ended and fails if too few survived, and
immediately reported `18/64 envs still flying`, which is how the next one was
found.

### 2.5 `flyaway` was killing 48 of 64 envs on step one: new, and the worst

At zero speed, before the policy had done anything.

`_resample_command` picks the next gate and teleports the drone to it, but
only `_update_command` publishes the gate *pose*, and Isaac Lab's step loop
runs `command_manager.compute()` **after** the termination and reward
managers. So for exactly one step after a reset, every consumer of the command
saw a pose belonging to the previous episode. On a freshly built env it was
still the zeros the buffer is initialised to, while the drone had been
scattered along a 22 m course: distances of 13–27 m against a 20 m `flyaway`
threshold.

**Fixed** by extracting `_refresh_gate_poses()` and calling it from both
paths. Step-one terminations went from 48 to 1.

Pre-existing, independent of the mass bug, and invisible by design: the only
symptom is episodes quietly ending early. It would have poisoned any run
started from a fresh env.

### 2.6 One env still spawns inside a gate

The surviving termination: 185 N of contact at x = 12.5, 1 in 64. Left alone
deliberately: a real but separate problem; whether it is one
bad gate or bad luck before changing where the aircraft is allowed to appear.

## 3. Two things about the tooling that cost more than the bugs

Both are now in the runbook's failure catalogue (`RUNBOOK_RUNPOD.md`), in
short: `SimulationApp.close()` hard-exits, so the smoke test's `sys.exit`
never ran and its buffered stdout was discarded, and a run that completed
looked exactly like the startup segfault; the fix is a flushed
`SMOKE_RESULT=` line that runners grep, because `$?` is not evidence. And a
tmux server started from an ordinary 1024-fd SSH session caps every pane it
spawns no matter what the script asks for; `scripts/pod/train_launch.sh`
checks both and refuses to start.

## 4. What this says about the plant, going in

Reward weights were tuned against an aircraft that could not fly, in episodes
that ended on step one. Both are now fixed. The balance those weights were
chosen under no longer exists, so the first curves after this will not
resemble anything previous; that is the fixes working, not new breakage.
Weights were not touched.
